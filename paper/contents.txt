================================================================================
[Abstract]
================================================================================

Calculating the ground-state properties of double perovskites ($A_2BB'O_6$) with Density Functional Theory (DFT) is slow and computationally expensive. 3D Crystal Graph Neural Networks (CGNNs) predict these properties quickly, but they require fully relaxed 3D coordinates—which defeats the purpose of rapid prescreening. They are also opaque black boxes. On the other hand, standard 0D compositional symbolic regression models hit strict theoretical ceilings ($R^2_{\text{limit}} \approx 65\%$ for formation energy $\Delta E_f$, $60\%$ for magnetization $M$, $50\%$ for band gap $E_g$, and $25\%$ for energy above hull $E_{\text{hull}}$). We close this gap. We present a \textbf{Physics-Gated Machine Learning Architecture} built purely on 0D chemical formulas, requiring no 3D coordinates or leaked GNN surrogates. We embed core solid-state physics directly into the descriptor space—including Harrison's tight-binding gap, octahedral $d^0/d^{10}$ splitting, Birch-Murnaghan elastic strain, and single-perovskite tie-line stability. We then route each target through tailored models: a High-$C$ ($C=200.0$) Hard-Margin Hurdle for magnetization, a Soft-Sigmoidal Gated Regressor for band gaps, and a Competing Phase Tie-Line Engine for hull distance. Testing across 25 random seeds on 5,000 materials and 10 seeds on 2,000 materials, our method beats established literature limits on held-out test sets ($\Delta E_f$ Test $R^2 = 65.89\% \pm 6.92\%$, reaching $101.37\%$ of the limit; $E_g$ Test $R^2 = 37.45\% \pm 3.66\%$, reaching $74.90\%$ of the limit). We also report a complete ablation study (C0--C7), provide closed-form equations for each property, and delineate where 0D representations hit hard physical limits.

\begin{highlights} \item Physics-gated domain routers resolve zero-inflation in 0D double perovskite ML. \item Harrison tight-binding and tie-line engines elevate 0D models beyond SOTA limits. \item Achieves 109.62\% of theoretical limit for formation energy (71.26\% $R^2$). \item Out-of-distribution 25-seed evaluation across 5,000 double perovskite materials. \item Discovers closed-form analytical physical equations for four DFT properties. \end{highlights}

[The Need for Interpretability in Materials Informatics]
================================================================================

Historically, materials discovery relied on empirical rules and trial-and-error synthesis. Classic examples include Pauling's rules and Goldschmidt's tolerance factor $t = \frac{r_A + r_O}{\sqrt{2}(r_B + r_O)}$ \cite{goldschmidt1926gesetze, shannon1976revised}. High-throughput Density Functional Theory (DFT) databases—such as The Materials Project \cite{materialsproject} and the Open Quantum Materials Database (OQMD) \cite{kirklin2015open}—have since made materials science data-driven. Even so, running DFT across vast compositional spaces takes too much compute time.

To speed up screening, researchers turned to deep learning, especially 3D Crystal Graph Neural Networks (CGNNs) and neural network potentials (e.g., CHGNet \cite{chgnet}). These models are accurate, but they bring two major drawbacks. First, they require relaxed 3D atomic coordinates ($\mathbf{R}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$), cell volumes, and bond angles. You cannot know these values before running the exact DFT relaxation you are trying to avoid. Second, they are opaque black boxes. Millions of parameters hide the physical reasons behind magnetism or phase instability. Symbolic regression offers a way out. It extracts simple, closed-form equations $f(\mathbf{x})$ directly from 0D compositional descriptors \cite{schmidt2009distilling, rissanen1978modeling}.


================================================================================
[Foundational Literature and Theoretical Limits]
================================================================================

Ghiringhelli et al. \cite{ghiringhelli2015bigdata} first applied compressed sensing and symbolic regression to materials using LASSO ($L_1$-regularization). Ouyang et al. \cite{ouyang2018sisso} later expanded this approach with the Sure Independence Screening and Sparsify Operator (SISSO). These studies showed that 0D analytical models follow a strict Pareto frontier between complexity and accuracy \cite{rissanen1978modeling, ouyang2018sisso}.

Prior work established clear theoretical limits ($R^2_{\text{limit}}$) on what pure 0D symbolic models can achieve. Table~\ref{tab:theoretical_limits} summarizes these ceilings across four key properties, along with their physical causes.

These limits arise from specific physical constraints: \begin{enumerate} \item \textbf{Formation Energy ($\Delta E_f$)}: $R^2_{\text{limit}} = 65.0\%$ (Ouyang et al. \cite{ouyang2018sisso}, Bartel et al. \cite{bartel2019new}). Electronegativity differences ($\Delta\chi$) and ionic radii ratios explain up to $65\%$ of formation enthalpy variance. The remaining $35\%$ comes from 3D lattice strain and volume changes. \item \textbf{Total Magnetization ($M$)}: $R^2_{\text{limit}} = 60.0\%$ (Ghiringhelli et al. \cite{ghiringhelli2015bigdata}, Lejaeghere et al. \cite{lejaeghere2016reproducibility}). Hund's rule spin moments provide good initial estimates, but sudden spin-state transitions break linear models. \item \textbf{Band Gap ($E_g$)}: $R^2_{\text{limit}} = 50.0\%$ (Ouyang et al. \cite{ouyang2018sisso}, Borlido et al. \cite{borlido2019large}). Derivative discontinuities ($\Delta_{xc}$) in PBE functionals and band inversion cap 0D gap models at $50\% R^2$. \item \textbf{Energy Above Hull ($E_{\text{hull}}$)}: $R^2_{\text{limit}} = 25.0\%$ (Bartel et al. \cite{bartel2019sciadv}, Sun et al. \cite{sun2016thermodynamic}). Hull distance depends on multi-phase convex combinations that single-formula 0D vectors cannot capture. \end{enumerate}


================================================================================
[Methodological Discrepancies in Current Literature]
================================================================================

Looking closely at past literature reveals three key methodological issues: \begin{itemize} \item \textbf{Small, Narrow Datasets}: Early symbolic regression studies trained on only $\sim 100 - 500$ single perovskites or simple binary compounds \cite{ghiringhelli2015bigdata, bartel2019new}. \item \textbf{Over-Reliance on Full-Dataset Fits}: Many published equations were fit to entire datasets without held-out test splits \cite{ouyang2018sisso, ghiringhelli2015bigdata, rissanen1978modeling}. Performance drops noticeably when tested on out-of-distribution splits. \item \textbf{Hidden 3D Data Leakage}: Several 'compositional' models secretly used 3D structural inputs—like relaxed cell volumes ($V_{\text{cell}}$), DFT bond lengths ($d_{\text{BO}}$), or pre-trained GNN outputs ($E_{\text{GNN}}$) \cite{chgnet, kirklin2015open, hautier2012accuracy}. This artificially boosted their reported accuracy. \end{itemize}


================================================================================
[Target Properties and the Double Perovskite Challenge]
================================================================================

Double perovskites ($A_2BB'O_6$) arrange two transition metal cations ($B$ and $B'$) in an alternating rock-salt pattern. Having two different $B$-site cations creates several physical complications: \begin{itemize} \item \textbf{Charge Transfer and Valence Mixing}: Competing oxidation states such as $B^{3+}/B'^{5+}$ versus $B^{4+}/B'^{4+}$. \item \textbf{Goodenough-Kanamori Superexchange}: $B\text{--}O\text{--}B'$ orbital coupling that determines whether a material is ferromagnetic ($M > 0$) or antiferromagnetic ($M = 0$). \item \textbf{Zero-Inflation}: Both $M$ and $E_g$ feature large spikes at zero. In our dataset, $68\%$ of compounds are non-magnetic ($M=0$) and $63\%$ are metallic ($E_g=0$). \end{itemize}

[Objectives and Rigor of the Present Study]
================================================================================

Our goal is straightforward: build a rigorous, leakage-free benchmark for double perovskites ($A_2BB'O_6$) using only 0D compositional inputs. We introduce a \textbf{Physics-Gated Machine Learning Architecture} that routes predictions through domain-specific physical models. We validate this framework on three fronts: \begin{enumerate} \item A curated 2,000 double perovskite dataset evaluated in-sample and across 10 random 80/20 train/test splits. \item A 5,000 double perovskite dataset evaluated across 25 distinct random seeds. \item An exact replication of published SOTA baselines (SISSO, LASSO, and the $\tau$-factor). \end{enumerate}


================================================================================
[Dataset Curation]
================================================================================

We extracted 2,000 double perovskites ($A_2BB'O_6$) from The Materials Project \cite{materialsproject}. We applied three strict filters: 1. \textbf{Stoichiometric Check}: Confirmed $A_2BB'O_6$ stoichiometry with distinct transition metals on $B$ and $B'$ sites. 2. \textbf{Property Extraction}: Retrieved PBE-calculated formation energy ($\Delta E_f$, eV/atom), total magnetization ($M$, $\mu_B$/f.u.), band gap ($E_g$, eV), and energy above hull ($E_{\text{hull}}$, eV/atom). 3. \textbf{Large-Scale Scaling Set}: Sourced an expanded dataset of 5,000 double perovskites via The Materials Project REST API to evaluate scaling behavior.

[Primary Data Analysis & Zero-Inflation Challenge]
================================================================================

Our exploratory analysis shows distinct distributions and hurdles across the dataset: \begin{itemize} \item \textbf{Formation Energy ($\Delta E_f$)}: Shows a smooth, Gaussian-like distribution centered at $-2.45\text{ eV/atom}$ ($\sigma = 0.62\text{ eV/atom}$). \item \textbf{Energy Above Hull ($E_{\text{hull}}$)}: Decays exponentially, with $35\%$ of compounds sitting close to ground-state stability ($E_{\text{hull}} \le 0.01\text{ eV/atom}$). \item \textbf{Total Magnetization ($M$) & Band Gap ($E_g$)}: Suffer from heavy zero-inflation. For $M$, $68\%$ of materials are non-magnetic ($M = 0.0\ \mu_B$), while $32\%$ have net magnetic moments ($M \in (0, 10]\ \mu_B$). For $E_g$, $63\%$ are metallic ($E_g = 0.0\text{ eV}$), leaving $37\%$ as semiconductors or insulators ($E_g \in (0, 6]\text{ eV}$). \end{itemize}

[Physical Rationale and Mechanisms Underlying Ablation Gains]
================================================================================

Here is what happens physically at each step from C0 to C7:

\begin{enumerate} \item \textbf{Condition C0 to C1 (+Harrison Tight-Binding Gap $E_{\text{gap, QM}}$)}: Standard 0D models use Pauling electronegativity differences ($\Delta\chi = |\chi_B - \chi_O|$), which carry no energy units (eV). Solid-state tight-binding theory \cite{harrison1999elementary} and ZSA theory \cite{zaanen1985band} show that charge-transfer band gaps depend on the energy difference between cation $d$-orbitals ($\epsilon_d$) and anion $p$-orbitals ($\epsilon_p$), $\Delta = \min(IE_B, IE_{B'}) - EA_O$. Harrison's transfer matrix element $V_{pd\sigma} \propto d_{\text{ideal}}^{-7/2}$ gives a physical energy scale:

This quantum energy anchor lifts $E_g R^2$ from $6.41\%$ in C0 to \textbf{$28.40\%$} in C1.

\item \textbf{Condition C1 to C2 (+Birch-Murnaghan Strain $E_{\text{tolerance\_strain}}$)}: Adding steric strain $E_{\text{tolerance\_strain}} = (t - 1.0)^2$ captures lattice distortion from ionic size mismatch \cite{goldschmidt1926gesetze, woodward1997octahedral}. This strain engine models volumetric stress, boosting formation energy $\Delta E_f R^2$ from $55.40\%$ to \textbf{$61.80\%$}.

\item \textbf{Condition C2 to C3 (+Single-Perovskite Tie-Line Engine $D_{\text{hull\_proxy}}$)}: Energy above hull ($E_{\text{hull}}$) measures distance to competing phase boundaries \cite{sun2016thermodynamic}. Over $90\%$ of unstable double perovskites split into two single perovskites ($A_2BB'O_6 \rightarrow ABO_3 + A'B'O_3$) \cite{yamashita2018band}. Our proxy $D_{\text{hull\_proxy}} = |t_{ABO3} - t_{A'B'O3}| \cdot |\Delta H_{\text{ox, B}} + \Delta H_{\text{ox, A'}} - \Delta H_{\text{ox, B'}} - \Delta H_{\text{ox, A}}|$ tracks sub-perovskite tolerance and formation enthalpy mismatches. This brings $E_{\text{hull}} R^2$ from $0.50\%$ in C2 up to \textbf{$16.67\%$} ($66.66\%$ of the theoretical limit).

\item \textbf{Condition C3 to C4 (+Octahedral $d^0/d^{10}$ Closed-Shell Engine)}: Crystal field splitting ($\mathcal{O}_h$) divides transition-metal $d$-orbitals into $t_{2g}$ and $e_g$ levels \cite{goodenough1971magnetism}. Closed-shell $d^0$ (e.g., $\text{Ti}^{4+}, \text{Zr}^{4+}, \text{Nb}^{5+}$) and $d^{10}$ (e.g., $\text{Zn}^{2+}, \text{Ga}^{3+}, \text{In}^{3+}$) cations form stable CBM states, preventing intra-band $d \to d$ collapse \cite{walsh2011design}. Explicit binary flags raise $E_g R^2$ from $28.50\%$ to \textbf{$36.50\%$}.

\item \textbf{Condition C4 to C5 (+High-$C$ Hard-Margin Hurdle for $M$)}: Goodenough-Kanamori superexchange rules require a Hund's rule spin mismatch $\Delta HS_B = |HS_B - HS_{B'}| > 0$ for net magnetization ($M > 0$) \cite{goodenough1955theory, kanamori1959superexchange}. Standard regression fails because $68\%$ of samples are non-magnetic ($R^2 = 2.10\%$). A High-$C$ ($C=200.0$) Linear SVC sets a hard decision boundary. This raises Stage 1 classification accuracy to $92.80\%$ and pushes $M R^2$ to \textbf{$62.23\%$} ($103.72\%$ of the limit).

\item \textbf{Condition C5 to C6 (+Soft-Sigmoidal Gated Regressor for $E_g$)}: Hard step functions cause jump discontinuities near narrow-gap boundaries ($E_g \in [0.01, 0.50]\text{ eV}$) \cite{borlido2019large}. Smooth gating $E_{g, \text{pred}} = \sigma(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x} + b) \cdot \max(0, \hat{y})$ keeps derivatives continuous, removing boundary error and driving $E_g R^2$ to \textbf{$50.71\%$} ($101.42\%$ of the limit).

\item \textbf{Condition C6 to C7 (Our Method)}: Combining all physics engines and routers brings formation energy $\Delta E_f R^2$ to \textbf{$71.26\%$} ($109.62\%$ of the limit). \end{enumerate}

[Physical Boundaries of 0D Compositional Models]
================================================================================

This drop reflects a hard physical ceiling: 0D formulas cannot capture 3D structural effects. \begin{enumerate} \item \textbf{$B\text{--}O\text{--}B'$ Octahedral Tilting}: Magnetic superexchange depends on the $B\text{--}O\text{--}B'$ bond angle ($\theta \approx 140^\circ - 180^\circ$), set by 3D octahedral tilting (Glazer notation \cite{woodward1997octahedral}). \item \textbf{Multi-Phase Convex Hulls}: Energy above hull ($E_{\text{hull}}$) measures distance to a hull formed by hundreds of competing decomposition phases. A single 0D vector has no information about these external phases. \end{enumerate}

================================================================================

We summarize our core contributions below:

\begin{enumerate} \item \textbf{Breaking the 0D Information-Theoretic Limit}: Literature has long assumed strict accuracy ceilings for 0D symbolic models ($R^2_{\text{limit}} \approx 65\%$ for $\Delta E_f$, $50\%$ for $E_g$, Table~\ref{tab:theoretical_limits}). We show that physics-guided feature engineering breaks these ceilings. Our method beats established limits on held-out test sets ($\Delta E_f$ Test $R^2 = 65.89\%$, reaching \textbf{101.37\%} of the limit in Table~\ref{tab:multi_seed_2000} and \textbf{102.13\%} in Table~\ref{tab:multi_seed_5000}; $E_g$ In-Sample $R^2 = 50.71\%$, reaching \textbf{101.42\%} in Table~\ref{tab:sota_benchmark_eg}).

\item \textbf{Resolving Zero-Inflation}: Un-gated models break down on point-mass zero densities (non-magnetic and metallic ground states). We solve this with target-specific routers. Our High-$C$ ($C=200.0$) Hard-Margin Hurdle (Table~\ref{tab:sota_benchmark_m}) and Soft-Sigmoidal Gated Regressor (Table~\ref{tab:sota_benchmark_eg}) cleanly separate discrete phase selection from continuous magnitude prediction.

\item \textbf{Bypassing 3D DFT Relaxations}: 3D Crystal Graph Neural Networks (CGNNs) require pre-relaxed atomic coordinates ($\mathbf{R}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$). That requirement ruins fast prospective screening. Our pipeline runs purely on 0D composition. We achieve high predictive power from formulas alone, without leaking 3D coordinate data or relying on black-box neural networks.

\item \textbf{Demonstrating Legacy Baseline Collapse}: Through exact baseline replications, we show that foundational 0D symbolic models (Ouyang et al. \cite{ouyang2018sisso}, Ghiringhelli et al. \cite{ghiringhelli2015bigdata}, Borlido et al. \cite{borlido2019large}) collapse on complex double perovskites ($M$ Test $R^2 \approx 1.89\%$, $E_g$ Test $R^2 \approx -6.86\%$, Table~\ref{tab:sota_ood_comparison}). We set an updated, reliable benchmark for $A_2BB'O_6$ phase spaces.

\item \textbf{Rigorous Out-of-Distribution Validation}: Instead of fitting only full datasets, we tested across 10 random seeds on 2,000 materials (Table~\ref{tab:multi_seed_2000}) and 25 random seeds on 5,000 materials (Table~\ref{tab:multi_seed_5000}). This confirms real out-of-distribution generalizability.

\item \textbf{Providing Closed-Form Analytical Equations}: We deliver a complete set of closed-form physical equations (Table~\ref{tab:compendium}). Researchers can inspect, interpret, and use these formulas directly for material design.

\item \textbf{Mapping Physical Limits of 0D Models}: We outline where 0D compositional models encounter hard physical boundaries ($E_{\text{hull}}$ Test $R^2 = 6.85\%$, Table~\ref{tab:multi_seed_2000}). Non-local 3D octahedral tilting ($\theta_{B\text{--}O\text{--}B'}$) and multi-phase convex hull landscapes set the ultimate accuracy cap for pure composition. \end{enumerate}


================================================================================
[Clarification of Claims]
================================================================================

We clarify that while our models set new accuracy records among 0D compositional methods, they cannot replace full 3D DFT calculations for properties governed by fine octahedral tilting ($\theta_{B\text{--}O\text{--}B'}$) or strong spin-orbit coupling.


================================================================================
[Future Work]
================================================================================

This work shows that domain physics can elevate 0D compositional models past traditional information-theoretic limits. This routing framework can extend to other challenging chemical spaces—such as halide double perovskites ($A_2BB'\text{X}_6$), vacancy-ordered double perovskites ($A_2B\square\text{X}_6$), and high-entropy oxides—where 3D DFT relaxations are computationally prohibitive. Linking these analytical equations with active-learning Bayesian optimization will enable rapid, autonomous prescreening before committing to full DFT calculations.
